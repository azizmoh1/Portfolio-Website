Soda Fountain Tee: CFD Mixing Study

University of Illinois Chicago · ME 396 Lab 4 · September 2026

The Design Question

Could a soda fountain tee deliver an acceptable average outlet temperature while also mixing the hot and cold streams uniformly? I used an energy balance to select inlet conditions for a 6°C mean, then compared the original tee with an extended outlet in ANSYS Fluent.

My Approach

I modeled steady, laminar flow with heat transfer and compared outlet temperature fields, engineering monitors and conservation checks. Two meshes assessed sensitivity in the original geometry; the revision retained the inlet conditions and increased the downstream length from 355.6 to 730.2 mm.

Results and Design Decision

Both designs produced a mean outlet temperature near 6°C, within the required 2–7°C band. Extending the outlet reduced the temperature range from 20.86°C to 17.65°C, a 15.40% improvement, but both exceeded the selected 1°C thermal-uniformity criterion. I rejected both designs as adequately mixed: an acceptable average concealed a persistent warm region.

What the Analysis Supports

Stable output monitors and small global conservation imbalances support the comparison, but residual targets were not met and the mesh checks do not establish formal mesh independence. Both streams were modeled as water; temperature uniformity is a surrogate for mixing, not a measurement of syrup concentration.

A next iteration would test geometry that splits and recombines the streams, then assess mesh refinement, convergence and pressure loss. Real syrup properties and concentration transport would require separate analysis.
