Systems Design Engineering Internship

Modular Hydronic Pump Package Platform for Commercial Chillers

I helped develop a ground-up modular product line of commercial hydronic pump skids, transforming custom one-off engineering into a standardized platform spanning 20+ capacity tiers. The project connected mechanical product design, hydronic systems, equipment packaging, BOM engineering, configuration management, and cost-modeling support into one repeatable workflow.

Role: Systems Design Engineering Intern Platform Scope: standardized modular product family covering 30 to 230 tons across 20+ configurations Core Disciplines: Mechanical Product Design, Hydronic Systems, Equipment Packaging, Configuration Management, BOM Engineering Tools: SolidWorks 3D assemblies, 2D drawings, configurations, and Microsoft Excel dynamic matrices
One-line pitch

From one-off skid designs to a configurable product platform.

Instead of treating each chiller installation as a fresh custom engineering job, the project created repeatable configurations that could be selected, documented, quoted, and expanded more efficiently.

Technical focus

Pumps, VFDs, tanks, piping, water quality, and skid structure.

The package consolidated chilled-water circulation, air/dirt separation, thermal buffering, water treatment provisions, electrical power, and frame integration.

Why it matters

Engineering work that affects design speed and product repeatability.

The strongest value was not a single CAD drawing; it was helping build a platform that reduces repetitive drafting, supports faster quoting, and improves consistency.

Engineering Context

The Problem: Custom Engineering Bottlenecks

Commercial skid deployments can become slow when every chiller package requires custom drafting, component selection, documentation, and quote support.

Custom job pressure

Previous deployments required one-off engineering and CAD drafting for unique chiller installations, creating long design cycles and slower quote response times.

Standardization objective

The goal was to transition from custom jobs to a repeatable product family that unified mechanical packaging, component selection, documentation, and generalized cost modeling.

Integrated package scope

The platform was designed as a turnkey skid that could bolt directly into air-cooled chiller frames while consolidating the core hydronic support equipment.

Subsystem Architecture

What the Platform Had to Integrate

The package had to behave like one coordinated mechanical product, even though it combined fluid, electrical, thermal, structural, and documentation requirements.

Motors & Drives1 HP to 5 HP motors at 1150 RPM and 1750 RPM paired with standardized VFD string-code logic for variable-speed flow regulation.
Thermal BufferingCBT buffer vessels spanning 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 850, 1040, and 1200 gallon model families.
Pressure ControlClosed-loop expansion tanks ranging from 23, 37, 53, to 79 gallons for hydronic pressure stabilization.

Fluid Manifolds5-inch grooved supply/return headers, dual Y-strainers, check valves, and butterfly isolation valves for serviceable flow paths.
Electrical PackageSingle-point power panel, VFD signal cabling, and branch circuit protection for centralized pump motor control.
Structural FrameHeavy-duty skid base, lifting points, frame bolt patterns, and chiller chassis interface planning.

Engineering Execution

Detailed Process

My work sat in the practical middle: understand the mechanical package, organize the configuration information, and help make the product family easier to repeat.

Step 01

Requirements Definition

Extracted performance criteria, interface envelopes, and capacity ranges from customer and equipment specifications.

Step 02

System Layout & Sizing

Mapped cooling load tiers to pump selections, motor/VFD requirements, buffer tank needs, expansion volumes, and separator flow thresholds.

Step 03

SolidWorks Modeling

Supported master CAD assemblies with skid structure, grooved piping networks, insulation solids, and electrical routing considerations.

Step 04

Manufacturing & Field Coordination

Considered dual-pump isolation loops, lifting points, service access, and bolt-pattern coordination with the chiller frame.

Step 05

Engineering Data Automation

Helped organize dynamic configuration spreadsheets linking mechanical sizing rules to BOM generation and generalized cost tracking.

Step 06

Technical Documentation

Supported general arrangement drawing logic, 3D model exports, datasheet organization, and configuration submittal structure.

Challenges & Solutions

Where the Engineering Got Interesting

Packaging high-capacity components

Large configurations required buffer tanks up to 1200 gallons and dual-pump layouts inside strict width and height envelopes.

Solution: Used isometric spatial planning to organize equipment placement, route grooved headers, and preserve service access without exceeding base boundaries.

Eliminating redundant one-off engineering

Custom skid quoting required repetitive manual design work and non-standardized data management.

Solution: Built platform logic around tonnage-driven selections, component models, hardware part numbers, and budgetary cost columns.

Coordinating multiple disciplines

Fluid piping, VFD controls, power routing, insulation, water quality hardware, and structural constraints could conflict physically.

Solution: Treated insulation boundaries, conduit paths, and equipment access as real packaging constraints instead of afterthoughts.
